Quantum engineering in the solid state

We study quantum coherence in the solid state, from the microscopic mechanisms of loss and decoherence to the design of hybrid quantum systems that connect distinct quantum degrees of freedom. Our current program brings together superconducting circuits, quantum defects, nanomechanics, and hybrid quantum systems.

Past Research

Color centers and solid-state spin defects

Color centers and solid-state spin defects provided the foundation for our approach to coherence, materials, and quantum interfaces. Earlier work focused on engineering optically active spin defects for quantum information and networking. A major theme was the neutral silicon-vacancy center (SiV0) in diamond: stabilizing its charge state and optical transitions, resolving its bound-exciton structure, and developing optical and microwave methods for spin initialization, coherent control, and readout. Related studies examined photoactivated carrier transport and surface control in diamond, telecom-band emitters, new long-lived diamond defects, and—more recently—spin–photon interfaces and nuclear-spin registers based on defects in silicon.
